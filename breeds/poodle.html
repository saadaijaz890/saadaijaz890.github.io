Poodle Anxiety: Complete Calming Guide

Poodles are smart, active, people-oriented dogs. Some Poodles become anxious when left alone or during storms, but the breed label alone does not decide that. This guide explains the signs, how to tell boredom from separation anxiety, practical steps for Toy, Miniature and Standard Poodles, and when to involve your vet.

By the AnxietyFreePups teamLast updated November 26, 20269 min read
Quick answer: Do Poodles Get Separation Anxiety?

Some Poodles do, but we have not found reliable evidence that Poodles as a breed are more prone to separation anxiety than other dogs. The AKC describes Poodles as “very people-oriented” and very active dogs who need good exercise every day, so a Poodle with little activity or little practice being alone may struggle when you leave. The ASPCA notes that separation anxiety signs, such as barking, chewing at exits, accidents and escape attempts, happen when a dog is left alone or separated from their person, not when you are home.

  • Watch for: Distress that starts as you prepare to leave or only happens when your Poodle is alone.
  • Rule out health first: Food refusal, sudden behavior change, new house soiling, or shaking for no clear reason means calling your vet early.
  • Start small: Daily exercise and brain games, plus short, calm absences your dog can handle without panic.

Are Poodles Prone to Anxiety?

Not as a rule. Poodles come in three AKC varieties (Toy, Miniature and Standard), and the AKC calls them “eager, athletic, and wickedly smart dogs of remarkable versatility.” We have not found good research showing that Poodles are more anxious than other breeds, so treat your own dog’s history, health, routine and early experiences as more important than the breed label.

Intelligence does not cause anxiety. A bright, active dog with too little to do can get frustrated or bored, though, and boredom behavior can look like anxiety. The AKC notes that Poodles of any size need good exercise every day and enjoy keeping busy.

Is It Separation Anxiety or Boredom?

The AKC describes separation anxiety as extreme stress from the time a dog is left alone until their person returns, and quotes behaviorist Patricia McConnell comparing it to a panic attack (AKC — Separation Anxiety in Dogs). Signs include pacing, whining, barking or howling, chewing or digging at doors and windows, accidents, drooling, and escape attempts.

  • More likely separation anxiety: Distress starts as you get ready to leave, happens only when your Poodle is alone, and your dog may not eat or play while you are gone.
  • More likely boredom or under-exercise: Your dog chews or barks but seems relaxed, eats treats while alone, and the behavior improves with more exercise and brain games.
  • Find out: Record your dog while you are out before choosing a fix.

For the full step-by-step plan, see our separation anxiety guide and Toy vs Standard Poodle separation anxiety protocol. For general time limits, see how long a dog can be left alone.

Common Poodle Anxiety Triggers

1. Being Left Alone

Poodles that rarely spend time apart from their people, or that face a sudden schedule change, a move, or a family member leaving, may find being alone hard. Small dogs who are carried everywhere can get very little practice being alone, so build alone time gradually for every size.

2. Thunderstorms and Loud Noises

Some Poodles pace, pant, shake or hide during storms or fireworks, and owners often notice signs before thunder is audible. Why dogs fear storms is not fully understood: noise, flashes, pressure changes and static electricity have all been suggested, but none is proven as the main cause. See our noise phobia guide for a full plan.

3. Too Little Exercise or Mental Work

Without daily walks, play and training, an active Poodle may become restless, bark, or chew. That is a needs problem, not necessarily anxiety, but it can make it harder for an anxious dog to settle.

4. Grooming and Handling

The Poodle coat needs regular grooming. Clippers, dryers and being held still can worry some dogs. Short, positive handling sessions with treats help, and sudden sensitivity to touch should be checked by your vet for pain or skin problems.

Plan ahead: If your Poodle reacts to storms or departures, start practice on calm days, keep each step small enough that your dog stays relaxed, and go back a step if you see panting, pacing or hiding.

How Can I Help My Anxious Poodle?

Practice alone time in small steps: Start with absences short enough that your Poodle stays relaxed, then lengthen them slowly. Keep departures and returns low-key.

Create a storm safe space: Choose a quiet interior room with a comfortable bed, background noise, and a stuffed toy. Practice using it on calm days so it becomes a relaxing place.

Desensitize to storm sounds: Play recorded storm sounds very quietly while your Poodle eats or plays, and raise the volume only over many sessions. If your dog shows fear, lower the volume. Recordings do not copy every part of a real storm, so keep your safe-space plan ready too.

Meet daily needs: Daily walks, play, swimming or retrieving games, and short training sessions help a busy Poodle settle. Exercise helps but does not fix true separation anxiety by itself.

Do not punish fear: Comforting a scared dog is fine. Punishment can make fear worse.

Know when to call your vet: Contact your vet early if your Poodle stops eating, injures themselves trying to escape, has accidents, or changes behavior suddenly. Severe noise fear or separation anxiety may need medication prescribed by a vet alongside behavior work; see our questions to ask your vet.

Frequently Asked Questions: Poodle Anxiety

Do Poodles get separation anxiety?
Some Poodles do, but we have not found reliable evidence that Poodles as a breed are more prone to it than other dogs. The AKC describes Poodles as very people-oriented and very active, so a Poodle with little exercise or little practice being alone may struggle. True separation anxiety signs happen when your dog is alone, not when you are home. Record your dog while you are out, build alone time gradually, and call your vet early if your dog stops eating or panics.
Are Poodles prone to anxiety?
Not as a rule. Poodles are smart and active, and a bored or under-exercised Poodle may bark, chew or seem restless, which can look like anxiety. Your dog's history, health, routine and early experiences matter more than the breed label. Sudden anxiety or behavior change deserves a vet check to rule out pain or illness.
Do Poodles have worse thunderstorm anxiety than other breeds?
We have not found reliable evidence that Poodles fear storms more than other dogs. Noise fear is common in dogs of many breeds, and its exact causes are not fully understood. Start desensitization on calm days, give your Poodle a quiet safe space, and ask your vet about medication if your dog panics during storms.
What size Thundershirt for a Standard Poodle?
Measure your Poodle's chest girth behind the front legs instead of choosing by variety or weight. On our ThunderShirt size chart, Medium fits a 21–25 inch chest, Large 25–30 inches and XL 30–37 inches, so many Standard Poodles fall between Medium and XL depending on build. Toy and Miniature Poodles usually need the small sizes. If your dog is between sizes, ThunderShirt recommends the smaller size for the Classic.
Are Toy Poodles more anxious than Standard Poodles?
There is no good evidence that one Poodle variety is more anxious than another. Any size can struggle with being alone. Small dogs are sometimes carried everywhere and get little practice being alone, so build short, calm absences into the routine for Toys, Miniatures and Standards alike.
Why does my Poodle shake during storms?
Shaking, panting, pacing and hiding are common signs of fear during storms. Noise, flashes and pressure changes may all play a part, but the exact cause is not fully understood. Offer a quiet interior room, stay calm, and never punish fear. If your Poodle shakes without a clear trigger, or also seems unwell or refuses food, contact your vet.
Best calming routine for a Poodle with storm phobia?
Set up a quiet interior room with a comfortable bed, background noise and a stuffed toy, and practice using it on calm days so it feels relaxing. When a storm is forecast, settle your Poodle there before fear starts. A well-fitted Thundershirt may help some dogs, but evidence is limited. For strong fear, ask your vet about medication alongside desensitization.

Dog Separation Anxiety When You Work From Home: Practicing Alone Time While You're In the House

Build calm alone time into your work-from-home day with a settle spot, gentle gate practice, short departures and camera checks for your dog.

By the AnxietyFreePups teamLast updated 27 Oct 202611 min read
Quick answer

Build comfortable alone-time practice into quiet moments of your workday, starting with a few seconds if needed. Use a camera, increase time only while your dog stays relaxed, and arrange company for absences they cannot yet manage.

  • Let your dog settle near your desk while building independence gradually.
  • Practice office-door and real departures below your dog's stress level.
  • Plan meetings around your dog's needs and current ability to cope.

Working from home can make life with a dog easier: you are nearby for walks, water refills and company. But if your dog rarely experiences an empty house, even a quick trip outside may feel unfamiliar or difficult.

You do not need to stop enjoying your dog's company to help them feel safer alone. We are dog owners, not vets or trainers. The aim is to use small, manageable moments in your day while making sure your dog gets the care and support they need.

Why working from home can make alone time harder

A dog who spends most days with someone nearby may have few opportunities to practice being alone. That does not mean working from home causes separation anxiety, or that a dog who follows you has it.

The important question is what happens when access to you changes. Some dogs rest comfortably outside the office. Others become upset when a door closes, and some settle while you are elsewhere in the house but struggle as soon as you leave it.

Barking, howling, whining, pacing, panting, drooling, scratching at exits or toileting during absences can point to distress. A recording helps you see when those behaviors start and whether they fade or intensify.

Settling while you work

Begin by making the workday comfortable for both of you. A suitable walk or sniffing activity, toilet break and breakfast before focused work can help your dog rest. Their needs depend on age, health and energy; the aim is to meet those needs, not exhaust them.

Place a familiar bed or mat near your desk if that is where your dog relaxes. Another dog may prefer a quiet spot outside the office. Offer water and keep cords, headphones and office supplies out of reach.

Notice and calmly reward your dog when they choose to settle. You might place a small treat on the mat or offer a familiar food toy. Affection and company are fine too; your dog does not need to spend the whole day apart from you.

A toy can be useful during a work block, but supervise new toys first. Once the food is gone, check whether your dog remains comfortable rather than assuming the activity has taught independence.

Closed office door or gate practice

Introduce the area while you are home and available. A dog-proofed room with a comfortable bed and water may work, or your dog may prefer to remain near your desk while you build confidence.

If using a gate, begin with it open. Once your dog is comfortable nearby, briefly close it while staying close and reopen it before worry develops. A closed office door should be introduced just as gradually.

Watch for gate pushing, climbing, chewing, scratching, persistent whining or pacing. These may mean the barrier itself is difficult. Return calmly and reassess; do not leave a panicking dog shut behind it.

Our dog gate vs exercise pen guide compares room-level setups. A barrier can help manage space, but it does not treat separation anxiety.

Video calls and meetings

A meeting is not the time to test a difficult new separation. If your dog normally rests beside you, letting them stay in the office may be the easiest option while you take the call.

Before the meeting, offer a toilet break and suitable activity. Set up a comfortable spot away from desk cables. If your dog safely enjoys a familiar food toy, you can offer it before the call; check that it is appropriate to use without continuous supervision.

For longer meetings, arrange another household member or sitter if your dog needs more attention than you can provide. A closed door is an option only after your dog has learned to relax with it for the relevant period.

If your dog becomes distressed during a call, help them rather than waiting for quiet. Afterward, adjust the next meeting plan. Your dog's need for support does not make them a bad coworker.

Tiny real absences during the workday

Practice leaving the home separately from being behind an office door. The change in context may matter: a dog who can relax while you are in the next room may still worry when the front door closes.

Choose a quiet moment when you can monitor video and return immediately. At first, your practice might be opening and closing the door, then stepping outside for a few comfortable seconds.

As your dog progresses, a brief trip to collect mail or take out the trash may become possible. A walk to the corner is a later step only if its entire duration is within your dog's demonstrated comfort. Household tasks are not targets your dog must reach by a certain date.

The ASPCA's separation anxiety advice describes treatment as helping a dog tolerate being alone by gradually getting used to short absences. While you work on it, the ASPCA suggests avoiding leaving the dog alone except during training, for example by having a family member, friend or sitter stay with them, or using doggy daycare.

Keep keys and shoes low-key, and sometimes handle them without leaving. Our departure cues guide explains how to introduce that practice gently.

Work-from-home alone-time checklist

  • Give your dog a suitable toilet break before focused work.
  • Provide appropriate movement and sniffing opportunities.
  • Keep fresh water accessible.
  • Set up a familiar, comfortable resting spot.
  • Remove office cords and chewable hazards.
  • Check that gates or doors are safe and familiar.
  • Plan meetings around your dog's current needs.
  • Arrange another carer for gaps your dog cannot handle.
  • Choose a quiet time when you can monitor practice.
  • Start the camera before moving away or leaving.
  • Keep the practice within your dog's comfort.
  • Return calmly if worry appears.
  • Note what happened before choosing the next step.
  • Supervise new food toys before using them during work.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not shut the office door for a long meeting before your dog is comfortable with it. Being in the house does not prevent distress if your dog cannot reach you.

Avoid using every break to increase difficulty. Repeat easy steps, and do not leave for a longer errand simply because one short session went well.

Never punish vocalizing, scratching or accidents, use bark-control devices, or leave a distressed dog to cry until quiet. Never shut a panicking dog in a crate, pen or room and leave.

Do not assume a food toy, a camera or a tired dog can safely manage an absence beyond their limit. Our how to stuff a KONG guide offers enrichment ideas, while the working full time guide discusses arranging care around work.

When to get extra help

Dogs Trust's separation anxiety advice explains that changes in behavior are often influenced by physical health, so see your vet if your dog's behavior has recently changed.

Contact your vet promptly about injuries, escape attempts, persistent food refusal or other signs of illness. A qualified, reward-based behavior professional can help if your dog panics immediately, struggles even with tiny steps or cannot safely use a barrier.

Arrange a family member, friend, sitter or suitable daycare for absences beyond your dog's comfort. Our care options guide can help you compare arrangements.

Bottom line

Your dog can enjoy being near you while learning that small moments apart are safe. Use comfortable resting spots, brief practice you can monitor and realistic meeting plans. Build real departures gradually and arrange company whenever your dog cannot yet cope.

Frequently Asked Questions: Separation Anxiety When You Work From Home

Why does my dog have separation anxiety when I work from home?
Working from home does not automatically cause separation anxiety, but your dog may have had little recent practice being alone. Some dogs also become distressed for other reasons. Record short absences and ask your vet about recent behavior changes.
How do I give my dog alone time when I work from home?
Start with a moment your dog can handle, such as moving away from their bed or briefly stepping through an inside doorway. Build gradually and use a camera when you cannot see them. Arrange company for any longer absence they cannot manage.
Should my dog be in my office while I work?
They can be if that is comfortable and safe for both of you. A familiar bed near your desk can provide a good resting place while you practice small steps at other times. Your dog does not need to be shut away to learn.
What do I do with my dog during video calls?
Meet their toilet and activity needs beforehand and let them rest in a familiar spot. A supervised introduction to a food toy may help some dogs settle. If they need more support during a long meeting, arrange another carer rather than testing a difficult separation.
How long can I leave my dog while working from home?
Use the duration your dog has demonstrated they can handle comfortably, along with their age, health and toilet needs. For a dog with separation distress, that may be only seconds at first. Build gradually and arrange company for longer gaps.

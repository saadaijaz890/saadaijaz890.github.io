Separation Anxiety After Vacation, Holidays or Working From Home: How to Help Your Dog Readjust

Help your dog readjust after vacation, holidays or working from home with a gentle two-week plan, familiar routines and support for your first days back.

By the AnxietyFreePups teamLast updated 24 Oct 202610 min read
Quick answer

Bring back familiar routines and practice short absences before normal workdays resume, increasing time only while your dog stays relaxed. Use a camera and arrange care for longer gaps; sudden changes or severe distress need a vet’s attention.

  • Start small, even if your dog previously managed longer absences.
  • Restore familiar walk, meal and sleep times before your return.
  • Arrange company when necessary absences exceed your dog’s current comfort level.

After weeks of shared breakfasts, midday walks and company on the couch, your first morning back can feel surprisingly difficult for your dog. A dog who previously napped through errands may follow you closely, watch your shoes or whine at the door.

You have not done something wrong by spending time together. Many dogs readjust with support, while others develop distress that needs a more gradual plan. We are dog owners, not vets or trainers, and the products below are chosen from research, not from personal testing. This guide helps you observe what changed and organize the next steps.

Why dogs struggle after a long time together

During holidays, sick leave, parental leave or working from home, dogs may have fewer chances to practice being alone. Walks, meals and sleep can shift too. Returning to the old schedule changes several things at once.

If your dog stays in a kennel while you travel, our guide to boarding a dog with separation anxiety explains how to plan a trial stay, drop-off day and the first days home.

Dogs Trust’s separation anxiety advice explains that changes in working hours, walks, feeding and time together can leave dogs unsettled and seeking comfort. That helps explain why a familiar workday may suddenly feel unfamiliar.

The ASPCA also lists an abrupt change in when or how long a dog is left alone as one of the situations linked with separation anxiety, for example going from working at home all day to a job that means long hours away.

Your dog’s previous ability is useful background, but their current behavior should guide your next absence.

Readjusting or separation anxiety?

Clinginess alone does not establish separation anxiety. Watch what happens after you leave, including body language and whether worry settles or builds. These clues guide your response; they cannot provide a diagnosis.

Before you go back: the week before

Start a few days or one to two weeks before your return, when possible. Gradually restore the schedule your dog will actually have: morning walks, meals, daytime rest and evening attention.

Record an easy practice departure to find out where you are starting. Do not test your dog’s old maximum. If leaving the room already causes worry, begin there.

Book care early and try a handover before the first workday. If your return is tomorrow, prioritize cover and begin practice afterward. You do not need to squeeze a training program into one evening.

Week-before checklist

Separation Anxiety After Vacation, Holidays or Working From Home: How to Help Your Dog Readjust: week-before checklist

  • Write down your return date and door-to-door absence.
  • Include commuting time and possible delays.
  • Gradually restore normal morning walk times.
  • Bring meals toward the usual workday schedule.
  • Protect regular sleep and daytime rest.
  • Choose a familiar, comfortable resting area.
  • Check that water remains accessible.
  • Remove cords, rubbish and swallowable objects.
  • Set up a camera with a useful view.
  • Record an easy departure without pushing for duration.
  • Note the earliest signs of worry.
  • Arrange care for gaps your dog cannot manage.
  • Practice a handover with that carer.
  • Save a backup carer’s contact details.

Your first day back

Allow time for a walk with sniffing, a potty opportunity and a calm transition afterward. A frantic exercise session immediately before departure can leave some dogs wound up.

Use the resting area and equipment your dog already knows. Offer a familiar food toy if appropriate, then leave with a brief, calm goodbye.

Have the carer arrive before your dog’s comfortable limit is reached. If that limit is very short, arrange an overlapping handover so there is no unsupported gap.

Check the camera when you can act on what you see. If distress builds, return or contact your arranged helper. A camera is information, not protection by itself.

Rebuilding alone time at home

Begin with your dog’s existing bed or mat. Quietly reward them for choosing to rest there while you read, prepare food or work nearby. They should remain free to move.

Next, take a step away and return. Progress to a brief room-to-room gap if your dog stays comfortable. A gate can help some dogs, but introduce it gently; stop if they climb, scratch or become tense.

Work on duration, distance or barriers separately. Do not suddenly combine all three.

If keys or shoes trigger worry, practice those actions without leaving. Our Departure Cues guide covers that separate task.

Cover for the first days

Dogs Trust also advises avoiding absences longer than your dog can cope with while addressing separation problems, using help from friends, family, sitters or daycare.

Choose company your dog actually enjoys. Trial daycare before relying on it, and introduce a sitter gradually. Some dogs relax with almost any familiar person; others need more support with handovers.

A dog walker can break up a comfortable day, but a brief visit cannot cover panic before or after it. For longer support, compare our Care Options guide and Working Full Time guide.

Is it getting better or worse?

Keep short notes alongside recordings: absence length, first sign of worry, whether your dog rested and what changed that day.

Improvement may mean comfortable resting during the same short absence, easier departure preparations or less time watching the door. Longer absences only count as progress when your dog remains comfortable.

Step back if worry starts earlier, lasts longer or includes new signs. Food eaten quickly does not cancel out distress afterward, and silence does not necessarily mean relaxation.

Compare several similar departures rather than one good or bad day. Our recording guide helps you capture useful footage.

If you work from home part of the week

Keep walks, meals and rest reasonably similar on home and office days. Include gentle independence practice on home days so every separation does not signal a long office absence.

You can still enjoy your dog’s company while working. There is no need to shut them away for an office-length stretch as rehearsal.

Arrange cover for office days based on current observations. A successful short errand on Tuesday does not establish readiness for Wednesday’s commute and workday.

Planning ahead for next time

During future holidays, preserve occasional easy alone moments if your dog is comfortable with them. Maintain some independent rest and keep familiar care relationships active.

Before the next return, review your schedule and restart short practice. Travel, visitors, disrupted sleep or other changes may affect what your dog can manage.

If a previous transition caused significant distress, organize professional support before another change rather than waiting for the first difficult workday.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not jump straight back to your dog’s previous longest absence. Their current comfort may be different.

Avoid suddenly withdrawing affection or rejecting a dog who seeks reassurance. Keep your relationship kind while creating manageable opportunities for independence.

Do not use punishment, bark collars or prolonged crying as training. Avoid forcing a crate or pen on a dog who panics in it.

Food toys, exercise and background sound can support a routine, but none makes an overwhelming absence manageable by itself.

When to get extra help

Contact your vet for a sudden behavior change, especially alongside appetite, sleep, mobility or toilet changes. A change after vacation can still have a medical cause.

Seek prompt veterinary advice for escape attempts, injury, persistent food refusal or severe distress. Active injury, collapse or breathing difficulty needs urgent care.

If difficulties continue after a couple of weeks, involve your vet or a qualified, reward-based behavior professional experienced in separation problems. Get help sooner when distress is intense; two weeks is not a waiting requirement.

Frequently Asked Questions: Separation Anxiety After Vacation or Working From Home

Why is my dog clingy after vacation?
Your dog may be adjusting to changes in company, walks, meals or rest after a period together. Clinginess alone does not establish separation anxiety, so watch what happens during short absences. A sudden or pronounced change is worth discussing with your vet.
How long does it take a dog to readjust after the holidays?
There is no fixed timetable: some dogs settle with routine and brief practice, while others need longer support. Use the two-week plan as a review framework rather than a deadline. Seek help sooner for severe distress, or if difficulties continue after a couple of weeks.
Can working from home cause separation anxiety?
Working from home does not automatically cause separation anxiety, and spending time with your dog is not a mistake. A later schedule change may reveal that being alone has become difficult. Short practice and care cover can make the transition more manageable.
How do I prepare my dog for me going back to the office?
Start before your return by restoring familiar routines and recording short, comfortable departures. Increase absences only while your dog stays relaxed, and arrange care for longer gaps. Include commuting time and delays when planning coverage.
Should I get a second dog to help?
Another dog may offer companionship, but it may not relieve distress about your departure. Adding a dog also brings new responsibilities and possible stress. Build a gradual plan and reliable care cover rather than adopting primarily to solve this problem.

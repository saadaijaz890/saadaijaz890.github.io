ThunderShirt Size Guide: Chart, Breed Picks & Fit Checks

Choosing the right ThunderShirt size starts with chest girth, not breed or weight alone. Use this chart, fit checks, and breed starting sizes to order with confidence.

Choosing the right ThunderShirt size is one of the most important steps before using a calming wrap for your dog. A ThunderShirt that fits properly should feel secure and comfortable without restricting normal movement. Since dogs come in many different shapes and builds, the best size is based on your dog's chest measurement, not just breed, weight, or appearance.

Many owners choose a size by guessing from their dog's weight or by copying the size another dog of the same breed uses. However, two dogs of the same breed can have very different body shapes. A slim Labrador and a broad-chested Labrador may need different sizes.

This ThunderShirt size guide explains how to measure your dog, understand the size chart, check the fit, and make a confident choice.

How to Measure Your Dog's Chest Girth

Chest girth is the most important measurement when selecting a ThunderShirt. Weight can help as a rough reference, but body shape and chest size determine the best fit.

  1. Use a soft measuring tape. A flexible sewing tape works best. Avoid a stiff ruler because it will not follow your dog's body shape accurately.
  2. Find the widest part of the chest. Wrap the tape around your dog's rib cage, behind the front legs, around the deepest part of the chest. Keep the tape level all the way around. This measurement is your dog's chest girth.
  3. Keep the tape snug but not tight. The tape should touch your dog's fur and body without pressing into the skin. Do not leave extra space, because loose measurements can lead to a size that is too large.
  4. Measure while your dog is standing. A standing position gives the most accurate chest measurement. Dogs sitting or lying down may have a different chest shape.
  5. Record the measurement. Write it down in inches and compare it with the chart below.

What If Your Dog Is Between Two Sizes?

One of the most common questions in a ThunderShirt size guide is whether to size up or size down. If your dog's measurement falls between two sizes, the manufacturer recommends choosing the smaller size.

Between sizes: Follow the manufacturer and size down — do not automatically size up for “extra pressure.” A wrap that is too loose may not provide the intended snug fit. Re-check the measurement, then use the two-finger fit test after fastening.

A ThunderShirt works by providing gentle, consistent pressure around your dog's body. A dog measuring slightly above a size boundary should be checked carefully, but avoid automatically choosing a larger size unless your dog's chest measurement clearly requires it.

Remember that the goal is not a tight garment. The goal is a comfortable, secure fit.

ThunderShirt Fit Checks

After putting on your ThunderShirt, spend a few minutes checking the fit. A correct fit should look secure while allowing your dog to move naturally.

The Two-Finger Test

Slide two fingers between the ThunderShirt and your dog's body. The wrap should feel comfortably snug, and you should not need to force your fingers underneath. If you cannot fit two fingers comfortably, it may be too tight. If you can easily fit several fingers or see noticeable space, it may be too loose.

Signs the ThunderShirt May Be Too Loose

  • It shifts around when your dog walks.
  • It hangs away from the body.
  • It twists to one side.
  • It leaves large gaps around the chest.
  • It will not stay positioned correctly.

Signs the ThunderShirt May Be Too Tight

  • Your dog has difficulty walking normally.
  • Your dog seems uncomfortable wearing it.
  • Shoulder movement looks restricted.
  • There are pressure marks after removal.
  • Your dog cannot lie down or change positions comfortably.

Common ThunderShirt Sizing Mistakes to Avoid

  • Choosing by weight only. A long, narrow dog and a short, broad dog can weigh the same but need different sizes.
  • Choosing based only on breed. Breed picks are starting points; always measure.
  • Buying too loose for comfort. A loose fit may not provide the secure feel the product is designed for.
  • Measuring the wrong area. Neck, shoulders, or belly measurements can result in the wrong size.

When the ThunderShirt Fit Does Not Work

Sometimes the issue is not the size itself. Dogs have different body shapes, and some may not respond well to a particular style of wrap. If it does not fit after measuring and adjusting, review whether the chest measurement was taken correctly, whether the wrap is positioned properly, whether your dog falls between sizes, and whether your dog has an unusual shape, such as a very deep chest, narrow waist, or broad shoulders.

Frequently Asked Questions About ThunderShirt Sizing

What is the best way to use a ThunderShirt size guide?
Measure your dog's chest girth first, then match it to the size chart. Weight and breed give general guidance, but chest measurement is the most important factor.
Should I size up or down for a ThunderShirt?
If your dog is between sizes, the manufacturer recommends the smaller size. It should fit snugly while allowing comfortable movement. Check with the two-finger test.
How do I measure my dog for a ThunderShirt?
Measure around the widest part of the chest, just behind the front legs, snug but not tight, with your dog standing.
What size ThunderShirt does a Labrador Retriever need?
Many Labs fall into XL under the current chart; a stocky upper end may need XXL. It depends on chest girth. Always measure rather than guessing from breed or weight.
What size ThunderShirt does a French Bulldog need?
Frenchies often have broad chests for their size. Many fall into Small, Medium, or Large. Do not choose on weight alone.
What size ThunderShirt does a Chihuahua need?
Most Chihuahuas fit XXS or XS. Measure chest girth to be sure.
Can a ThunderShirt be too tight or too loose?
Yes to both. Too tight restricts walking, sitting, or lying down; too loose shifts, twists, or gaps. Use the two-finger test and recheck the measurement.

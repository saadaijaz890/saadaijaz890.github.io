Do Calming Dog Beds Work? How to Choose the Right Size and a Washable Bed

Calming dog beds may help dogs settle comfortably, but they don't treat anxiety alone. Learn which style, size, and washable design suits your dog's needs.

By the AnxietyFreePups teamLast updated 15 Oct 20269 min read
Quick answer

Calming dog beds can provide a comfortable resting spot that helps some dogs settle, especially those who enjoy leaning against bolsters or burrowing into soft fill. They do not treat separation anxiety or noise fear on their own; fit, accessibility, routine, and individual preference matter.

  • Match the usable sleeping space to your dog's preferred resting position.
  • Check washing instructions for the exact size and model before buying.
  • Persistent restlessness, pain signs, or panic needs more than a different bed.

A fluffy donut bed looks inviting, but will your dog actually sleep in it? The answer depends less on the word “calming” than on how your dog rests, whether the bed supports comfortable movement, and where you put it.

We are dog owners, not vets, and these picks are a research-based assessment rather than our own product testing. This guide separates useful comfort features from anxiety claims and helps you choose a bed you can realistically keep clean.

Do calming dog beds work? What they can and can't do

A calming bed usually combines a soft sleeping area with raised edges. For a dog who enjoys curling tightly, resting their chin on something, or nesting into fabric, that arrangement can feel comfortable and sheltered.

The rim provides something to lean against. Soft fill allows some burrowing and repositioning. Those are practical design features, not evidence that a particular bed treats anxiety.

Evidence supporting calming beds as an anxiety treatment is limited. A dog sleeping in one does not establish that the bed changed their underlying fear.

A bed cannot independently treat separation anxiety, noise phobia, or fear of visitors. It works best within a routine: a familiar calm spot, appropriate activity, gradual alone-time practice, and professional help when distress is significant.

Keep choice in the picture. A dog who prefers stretching on a cool floor may dislike thick shag fabric. Refusing a donut bed does not mean your dog cannot learn to settle.

How we chose these picks

We considered sleeping position, usable space, entry height, fill or foam construction, cleaning access, and the consequences of damage.

Best Friends by Sheri's calming donut bed is our top pick for curlers who like leaning and burrowing. That is a preference-based recommendation, not a claim that it reduces anxiety better than other beds.

We also prioritized practical ownership questions. Can you remove the cover without wrestling with the filling? Will the bed fit your washer? Can you obtain a replacement cover?

For older or joint-sore dogs, support and access take priority over fluffiness. A comfortable appearance cannot tell you how a bed feels under your dog's weight.

How to choose the right size

Measure your dog while they lie in their usual sleeping position, from nose to base of tail, then add a few inches as a starting allowance. Also note the width occupied by their body and legs.

For a curled dog, measure the curled footprint rather than assuming standing body length describes the space they use. For a sprawler, include their extended legs and room to change position.

Compare those measurements with usable sleeping space. A round bed's advertised outside diameter includes its rim, which can leave a much smaller central area.

Size up if your dog regularly hangs off the sleeping area or cannot change position comfortably. Check each listing's chart; there is no universal calming-bed size system.

A bed that is too small may be ignored or leave your dog cramped. A much larger donut bed may lose the snug surrounding feel a curler enjoys, although some dogs prefer that extra space.

Washable beds: what to look for

“Washable” can mean the whole bed, only the cover, or selected components. Read the instructions for the exact model and size before buying.

A removable zip cover is useful for frequent washing, especially on large beds. Check whether the sleeping surface, base, and bolsters are accessible, and whether the inner cushions also need cleaning.

Whole-bed machine washing can work for smaller designs, but the bed must fit your machine without overloading it. Wet filling becomes bulky, and drying can take considerably longer than washing.

For Best Friends by Sheri, consult the manufacturer's washing instructions and your bed's care label. Do not assume every size or collection uses the same process.

Allow all layers to dry completely before reassembly. Follow the specified drying method; inappropriate heat or air-drying against instructions can damage some materials or affect the fill.

For accidents, look for a waterproof liner beneath the washable cover. A water-resistant base is not the same as protecting foam from liquid entering above.

Check zippers, seams, and stuffing regularly. Supervise a dog who mouths bedding, and remove the bed if they shred it or swallow material. No soft bed should be assumed chew-proof.

Your 7-day new bed routine

This routine is an introduction, not a deadline. Repeat an easier day whenever your dog seems unsure.

  1. Day one: Place the bed in a quiet, familiar area. Keep the existing resting option available, and allow sniffing without lifting or pushing your dog onto the new bed.
  2. Day two: Reward voluntary interest. Place a few small treats near or on the bed if appropriate, allowing your dog to leave freely rather than asking for a prolonged stay.
  3. Day three: Sit nearby during a naturally quiet period. Reward relaxed resting, then reduce interaction so the bed can become a place to switch off.
  4. Day four: Add a simple resting cue if useful. Say it as your dog approaches voluntarily, reward, and avoid repeatedly asking when they would rather rest elsewhere.
  5. Day five: Make ordinary movements nearby while your dog rests. Start with small changes, such as standing briefly, and reduce difficulty if your dog becomes watchful.
  6. Day six: If appropriate, include a very brief, comfortable separation. Observe directly or by camera; return before distress rather than expecting the bed to prevent anxiety.
  7. Day seven: Review preference, fit, warmth, and cleaning needs. Adjust location or bed type if necessary. Comfortable use matters more than completing every step.

Signs it's more than a bed problem

Do Calming Dog Beds Work? How to Choose the Right Size and a Washable Bed: signs it’s more than a bed problem

  • Restlessness or inability to settle appeared suddenly.
  • Your dog struggles to lie down, rise, or change position.
  • Nighttime pacing, crying, or confusion repeatedly interrupts sleep.
  • Departures trigger persistent barking, drooling, pacing, or escape attempts.
  • Storms or fireworks cause panic despite a comfortable retreat.
  • Your dog shreds and swallows bedding or guards the bed from people.
  • Appetite, thirst, toileting, breathing, or normal activity has changed.

Arrange a vet check for new symptoms, possible pain, or disrupted sleep. VCA's guidance on pain in aging dogs describes movement changes worth reporting.

For recurring fear or separation distress, involve a qualified behavior professional alongside your vet. Do not keep buying softer beds while a larger problem goes unaddressed.

Bottom line

Calming dog beds can be useful comfort purchases for dogs who enjoy their shape and texture. Choose sleeping space, support, accessibility, and realistic washing requirements before choosing a marketing label.

A donut bed suits many curlers; a low-entry supportive bed may suit a stiff senior better. Neither replaces gradual training or veterinary help when fear, pain, or persistent restlessness prevents comfortable rest.

Frequently Asked Questions: Calming Dog Beds

Do calming dog beds really work?
They can provide a comfortable, sheltered resting spot for dogs who enjoy bolsters and soft fill. Evidence for treating anxiety with the bed itself is limited, so judge usefulness by comfortable rest rather than expecting fear to disappear.
What size calming bed should I get for my dog?
Measure your dog in their usual sleeping position from nose to base of tail, add a few inches, and check occupied width. Compare that footprint with the listing's usable sleeping area, accounting for bolsters and room to change position.
Can you wash a calming dog bed?
Often, but some models allow whole-bed washing while others permit only removable covers to be washed. Follow the exact care label, check machine capacity, and dry all permitted components completely before use.
Are calming beds good for dogs with separation anxiety?
A familiar bed can be part of a comfortable resting setup, but it does not treat separation anxiety. Pair it with gradual alone-time practice and seek help if your dog panics, attempts escape, or cannot settle during absences.
Calming bed vs orthopedic bed which is better?
A soft donut bed may suit a comfortable curler, while an orthopedic bed may better suit a dog needing support or easier movement. Some beds combine supportive foam and bolsters, so choose according to your dog's comfort, mobility, and sleeping position.

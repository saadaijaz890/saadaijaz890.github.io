Getting Married With a Dog Who Has Separation Anxiety

Plan wedding-day and honeymoon care for your anxious dog with early bookings, trial stays, a clear handover, backup help and a calm return home.

By the AnxietyFreePups teamLast updated 1 Nov 202610 min read
Quick answer

Book familiar, suitable care for both the wedding and honeymoon, including daytime company and backup coverage. Keep ordinary routines and comfortable alone-time practice going before the wedding, then use trial stays to check the arrangement before you leave.

  • Choose wedding-day care according to your dog's comfort, not the photo opportunity.
  • Trial an overnight and weekend before relying on honeymoon care.
  • Keep updates practical, with someone local ready to respond.

Wedding planning can quietly reshape your dog's week: evening appointments, visitors, bags appearing and walks squeezed between tasks. Then the wedding arrives, followed by a honeymoon when both familiar people may leave together.

We are dog owners who researched this topic, not vets, trainers or product testers, and the products mentioned here were chosen from research, not from personal product testing. This guide focuses on keeping your dog's care dependable through the celebration, from the busy preparation weeks to a supported return home.

Why weddings are hard for a dog with separation anxiety

Your dog does not know what the wedding calendar means. They experience the practical changes: different schedules, less predictable company and unusual activity around the home.

Packing, guests and repeated outings may already make settling harder before the wedding day. The honeymoon then introduces a much longer separation and possibly a different caregiver or setting.

The ASPCA's page on separation anxiety describes separation anxiety as distress when dogs are left alone or separated from the people they're attached to, and explains that treatment focuses on gradually teaching the dog to be comfortable alone.

Wedding deadlines do not shorten that learning process. A dog who manages a brief absence is not automatically ready for long gaps between caregiver visits.

Plan according to how your dog actually behaves with the proposed carer, including when both of you leave.

The busy weeks before the wedding

Protect the basics first: meals, potty breaks, suitable walks and quiet rest. Put dog care on the shared calendar alongside appointments.

If preparation cuts into walks, ask a familiar helper to take over rather than repeatedly skipping them. Sniffing and ordinary activity meet daily needs, although extra exercise alone does not treat separation distress.

Continue short, comfortable alone-time practice. Record sessions and increase only while your dog remains relaxed. Avoid turning a rehearsal dinner or planning appointment into a sudden long training session.

Introduce packing and caregiver visits during ordinary moments where possible. Let your dog become familiar with the person who will handle wedding-day care.

If you are also combining households, use our new partner or roommate guide for that separate transition. Try not to introduce unnecessary changes to sleeping places or household rules during the final wedding rush.

Wedding day: should your dog be there?

For most dogs already distressed by separation, a busy wedding is unlikely to be the easiest care setting. Home with a familiar person is usually the simpler starting option to assess.

Your dog does not need to attend to be part of your life together. Consider crowds, music, unfamiliar handling, waiting periods and whether the dog can genuinely rest.

If attendance appears suitable, check with your venue and appoint a dedicated handler your dog knows. That person needs responsibility for the dog throughout—not competing ceremony or guest duties.

Plan a brief, manageable appearance and a quiet exit to a confirmed care arrangement. The handler should leave with the dog if they become uncomfortable, without waiting for photographs or the next event.

Never leave the dog alone in a car, outside or shut in an unfamiliar room while the celebration continues. If your dog cannot settle in the proposed arrangement, choose care away from the event.

Plan honeymoon care early

For a 1–2 week honeymoon, consider an in-home sitter your dog knows, trusted family or a boarding setting they genuinely handle well. Our care options guide compares these choices in more detail, and if you plan to bring your dog along instead, our traveling with an anxious dog guide covers hotels, rentals and visiting family.

Confirm both daytime and overnight care. An overnight sitter may have other commitments during the day, so agree on actual presence and cover any unsupported gaps.

Begin with introductions and a short handover while you remain nearby. If comfortable, arrange a trial overnight, followed by a trial weekend before the honeymoon. Do not progress simply because the dates are approaching.

Ask how your dog eats, sleeps and settles after you leave. A happy greeting when you return does not describe the whole stay.

For boarding, check staffing, time alone and responses to distress. Our boarding guide covers the fuller assessment; availability alone does not establish suitability.

Book backup help and agree what happens if travel is delayed. Check the current terms of bookings rather than assuming an extension or replacement will be available.

A handover sheet for the sitter

Write one clear summary with fuller records available separately. Include the dog's routine, current comfortable alone-time level and what the sitter should do if distress appears.

Describe behavior plainly. “Paces and scratches the door when left” is more useful than simply writing “anxious.”

Include food, water, potty needs, sleeping arrangements, triggers and safe handling. Explain which activities help and which barriers or interactions make things harder.

Name a local backup who can act if you are unavailable. Share veterinary contacts and ask your clinic how to authorize care while you're away.

Staying in touch while you're away

Agree on useful updates before departure. A morning message and evening summary may suit your arrangement, with immediate contact for concerns rather than waiting for the next scheduled update.

Ask about eating, drinking, potty breaks, sleep and the ability to rest. Brief video can add context, but it does not replace the sitter's observations.

Use cameras only with the caregiver's knowledge and agreed access. Watch for repeated pacing, panting, drooling, howling or exit-focused behavior.

If footage shows distress, contact the sitter and activate the local backup if needed. Do not continue watching a struggling dog while hoping the problem resolves itself.

Avoid assuming a call through the camera will comfort your dog. Some become more unsettled by hearing a person they cannot find. The care plan needs someone physically available to respond.

Settling back in after the honeymoon

Keep the reunion warm and unhurried. Offer a potty break, water and familiar company, then allow your dog to rest.

Ask the sitter for a concise summary before the handover ends. Note any changes in appetite, sleep, potty habits or comfort during departures.

Resume short, easy alone-time practice before assuming your previous duration still applies. Record sessions and keep company arranged for necessary longer absences if your dog struggles.

A new last name does not require changing familiar cues or routines. If a new home is also part of the wedding plans, separate optional changes where practical and prepare support for unavoidable ones.

Keep identification and veterinary contact details accurate if your information changes. Our separation anxiety after vacation guide provides a fuller re-entry plan; the immediate aim is a recognizable routine rather than another abrupt transition.

When to see the vet or a behavior professional

A dog with strong distress should see the vet before the wedding and honeymoon where possible. Share recordings and explain how the proposed care will work.

Contact your vet early if your dog refuses food, seems unwell or shows a sudden behavior change. Do not assume every symptom comes from missing you.

Persistent panic, escape attempts or self-injury need prompt help. Serious injury, collapse or breathing difficulty require emergency veterinary care. Our separation anxiety red flags guide explains which signs are urgent.

US emergency help: If your dog is injured, struggling to breathe, has collapsed, or may have swallowed something dangerous, call your vet or the nearest 24/7 emergency animal hospital now, and call ahead so the team can get ready. For suspected poisoning you can also call the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at (888) 426-4435, open 24 hours a day (a consultation fee may apply). Panic without injury, refusing food, or a sudden change in behavior: call your regular vet the same day. See our separation anxiety red flags guide for which signs are urgent.

A qualified reward-based behavior professional can help when caregiver trials remain difficult or your dog cannot settle with alternative company.

Never punish barking, howling, damage or accidents; review health, safety and the care arrangement instead. Never shut a panicking dog in a crate or room and leave.

Frequently Asked Questions: Weddings, Honeymoons and an Anxious Dog

What do I do with my dog during my honeymoon?
Arrange a familiar in-home sitter, trusted family caregiver or boarding setting your dog handles well. Confirm daytime and overnight coverage, complete suitable trials and prepare backup help. Drop-in visits or a camera alone are not the care plan.
Should my anxious dog come to our wedding?
Home with a familiar person is usually a simpler option for a dog already struggling with separation. Attendance needs a comfortable trial, venue confirmation, a dedicated handler and a quiet exit plan. Skip participation if the environment overwhelms your dog.
Is a sitter or boarding better for a dog with separation anxiety during a honeymoon?
It depends on where your dog actually settles and how much company each option provides. An in-home sitter preserves familiar surroundings, while some dogs cope well in an established boarding setting. Trial the arrangement and confirm all care gaps are covered.
How long before the wedding should I book a pet sitter?
Start looking once your dates are likely, leaving time for introductions, an overnight trial and a trial weekend. Availability varies, so early contact gives you more room to assess suitability. Introduce a backup rather than relying on a single booking.
Why is my dog acting out after our honeymoon?
Changed routines or difficulty during the separation may affect behavior after you return, but discomfort or illness can also play a part. Restore familiar care, review recordings and restart easier practice without punishment. Contact your vet early if your dog refuses food, seems unwell or shows sudden changes.

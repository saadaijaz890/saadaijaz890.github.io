Thunderstorm and Fireworks Anxiety in Pugs: How to Keep Your Dog Calm

Many dogs, Pugs included, tremble, pace, or hide when thunder rolls or fireworks start. Here is a source-backed plan to help your Pug through noisy nights, with the heat and breathing cautions that matter for a flat-faced breed, and how to tell noise fear from separation anxiety.

Quick answer: How do I calm my Pug during thunderstorms and fireworks?

Give your Pug a familiar safe spot away from windows, mask the noise with white noise or calm music, offer a stuffed chew or lick mat, and stay calmly nearby without punishing fearful behavior (AKC). Keep outside doors closed so a scared dog can’t bolt, and keep your Pug cool: the AKC says Pugs aren’t tolerant of hot weather, and the ACVS notes that breathing distress in short-faced dogs often follows excitement, heat, or humidity. For lasting change, practice gradual sound desensitization in the off-season. If your Pug panics, has trouble breathing, refuses food, or seems unwell, call your vet early; prescription options exist for noise aversion.

Why Are Pugs Scared of Thunder and Fireworks?

Noise fear isn’t unique to Pugs; any dog can develop it. In the AKC’s guide to thunderstorm anxiety, trainer Trista Miller points to loud sounds and dogs’ sensitive hearing, plus possible sensitivity to air-pressure changes and static electricity. The ASPCA adds that the trigger can be the sudden noise, the bright flash, or the change in barometric pressure, and sometimes more than one. Fireworks are loud, sudden, and unpredictable, which makes them hard for many dogs.

Common signs of noise fear include trembling or shaking, pacing, whining or barking, paw licking, hiding, destructive behavior, and attempts to escape (AKC). Pugs often head straight for their people: the AKC breed profile says a Pug “wants to be with his family.”

Is My Pug’s Panting From Fear or a Breathing Problem?

Panting is a normal stress sign, but in a Pug it deserves a closer look. The American College of Veterinary Surgeons (ACVS) lists the Pug among brachycephalic (short-headed) breeds prone to obstructed breathing, and says exercise intolerance, cyanosis (blue tongue and gums), and occasional collapse are common in affected dogs, especially after over-activity, excitement, or excessive heat or humidity. A frightened, overheated Pug on a warm Fourth of July night combines two of those triggers.

Call your vet right away if: your Pug’s breathing is loud or labored and doesn’t settle once things are quiet, their tongue or gums look blue or gray, or they collapse. Also call your vet early if fear comes with food refusal, vomiting, or your Pug seeming unwell, or if noise sensitivity starts suddenly; the AKC notes that dogs in pain may react fearfully to sounds that didn’t bother them before.

On noisy nights, keep the room cool (the AKC says Pugs are better off in air-conditioning when it’s hot), skip strenuous play when it’s warm or humid, and offer water.

How Can I Calm My Pug During a Storm or Fireworks?

1. Set Up a Safe Space

A familiar bed, covered crate, or quiet interior room away from windows works for many dogs (AKC). Only close a crate door if your Pug already relaxes in it: veterinary behaviorists writing for VCA Animal Hospitals warn that many dogs panic and could seriously injure themselves if confined during loud noises, so don’t confine your dog during fireworks or a storm unless you can monitor them.

2. Mask the Noise

Close windows and curtains, and play white noise, calm music, or the TV to muffle sounds from outside (AKC). See our guide to calming music for dogs.

3. Stay Calm and Offer Comfort

Stay calm yourself and never punish whining or destruction during a storm (AKC). Talking in a soothing voice and, if your Pug likes it, long, calming strokes can help. In the AKC’s guide to fears and phobias, AKC Chief Veterinary Officer Dr. Jerry Klein says constant pressure, such as a hand resting on your dog, is better than patting, and cautions that excited “good boy” praise during the scary moment can reinforce fearful behavior.

4. Give a Chew or Lick Outlet

A long-lasting treat, a stuffed hollow chew toy, or a lick mat gives some dogs something positive to focus on (AKC). Choose a size your Pug can’t swallow and supervise. Many scared dogs won’t eat, which is a sign the fear is too strong right now; don’t force it.

5. Prevent Escapes

Keep outside doors closed; the AKC notes that dogs can be injured or get lost trying to get away from frightening noises. The ASPCA warns that dogs who chew or swallow things when scared can get seriously ill or develop an obstruction, so keep an eye on your dog throughout. It’s also worth checking that your Pug’s ID tag and microchip details are up to date before a big fireworks night.

How Do I Desensitize My Pug to Loud Noises?

Gradual desensitization paired with counterconditioning is a commonly used behavioral approach for storm and fireworks fears (VCA), and the AKC says veterinarians and board-certified veterinary behaviorists recommend behavior modification as a first line of defense. A simple version, based on the VCA and AKC guidance:

  1. Start in a season when storms or fireworks are unlikely; VCA notes that an accidental full-volume exposure can erase progress.
  2. Teach your Pug to settle on a mat for treats.
  3. Play a recording of thunder or fireworks at a barely audible volume while your Pug relaxes, eats, or plays.
  4. Raise the volume only a little at a time, and only while your Pug stays relaxed. At any sign of fear, turn it down and go slower.

Expect this to take weeks or months, especially for adult dogs (AKC). For a full plan, see our noise phobia guide and the fireworks night protocol.

Is It Noise Fear or Separation Anxiety?

The ASPCA describes separation anxiety as distress triggered when a dog becomes upset because of separation from their guardians, the people they’re attached to. Noise fear is tied to the sound: your Pug trembles when the storm starts, even with you beside them. Separation anxiety is tied to your absence: distress when you leave, even on quiet days.

A Pug can have both, and loud noises while home alone can make things worse. On big fireworks nights such as July 4 and New Year’s Eve, plan to be home or have someone stay with your Pug. If you aren’t sure what happens while you’re out, record your dog when left alone. For Pugs who follow you everywhere, see why Pugs are so clingy and how long a Pug can be left alone.

When Should I Call the Vet About My Pug’s Noise Fear?

Don’t wait for it to pass on its own. According to the AKC’s Dr. Klein, phobias may worsen with time and rarely resolve on their own. Call your vet if your Pug panics, tries to escape, injures themselves, or if fear is spreading to other sounds. The AKC also advises seeing your vet first to rule out medical causes such as pain.

Your vet may suggest medication alongside training. The AKC notes that most drug therapies work best combined with behavior modification and aren’t an instant cure. One example is SILEO (dexmedetomidine oromucosal gel), a prescription gel approved by the FDA for noise aversion in dogs. Its FDA-approved label says not to use it in dogs with severe respiratory disease or stress due to extreme heat, which is one more reason medication decisions for a Pug belong with your vet. Don’t give your Pug any human medication unless your vet prescribes it.

Plan ahead: Check the forecast in storm season and mark July 4 and New Year’s Eve on your calendar. Set up the safe space, white noise, and chew before the noise starts.

Frequently Asked Questions: Pug Noise Anxiety

How can I calm my Pug during thunderstorms?
Give your Pug a familiar safe spot away from windows, play white noise or calm music to muffle the thunder, and offer a stuffed chew toy or lick mat if they will take it (AKC). Stay calm, never punish fearful behavior, keep outside doors closed so your Pug can’t bolt, and keep the room cool. If your Pug panics, keeps trying to escape, or has trouble breathing, contact your vet.
Are Pugs prone to noise anxiety?
We found no reliable data showing that Pugs are more noise-phobic than other breeds. Any dog can develop a fear of thunder or fireworks. The AKC says a Pug wants to be with his family, so many Pugs seek you out when they are scared. The AKC also notes that phobias may worsen with time and rarely resolve on their own, so it helps to act early.
Is my Pug’s heavy panting during fireworks dangerous?
It can be. Pugs are a brachycephalic (short-faced) breed, and the ACVS says dogs with brachycephalic syndrome often show exercise intolerance, blue tongue and gums, and occasional collapse, especially after over-activity, excitement, or excessive heat or humidity. Keep your Pug cool and calm. Noisy or labored breathing that doesn’t settle, blue or gray gums, or collapse needs a vet right away.
Do calming products work for Pugs?
Some help some dogs, but none is guaranteed. The AKC says steady pressure, such as an anxiety wrap or a hand on your dog, is better than patting, and evidence for pheromone products and calming chews is mixed. Treat any product as optional, follow the label, and ask your vet first, especially if your Pug has breathing problems or takes medication. Gradual sound training and, when needed, vet-prescribed medication do the main work.
Is noise anxiety the same as separation anxiety?
No. The ASPCA describes separation anxiety as distress triggered when a dog is separated from the people they’re attached to. Noise fear happens when the sound happens, even if you’re home. A Pug can have both, and fireworks while your Pug is home alone can be especially hard, so plan to be home or have someone with them on big fireworks nights.
